Types of lighting

Know your fittings

Each kind of light does a different job. Knowing what they're good at makes it easier to plan a room.

Pendants

Hang from the ceiling to light a table, island or the centre of a room.

Table lamps

Soft light at eye level for bedsides, side tables and cosy corners.

Floor lamps

Fill dark corners and give focused light beside a chair or sofa.

Smart bulbs

Change brightness and warmth from your phone or on a schedule.
The basics

Better light in three steps

01

Light in layers

Combine ambient light to fill the room, task light for reading or cooking, and accent light to add mood. One ceiling light alone tends to feel flat.

02

Switch to LED

LED bulbs use up to 85% less energy than old incandescent bulbs and can last 15 years or more, with warm tones that look just as cosy.

03

Add dimmers

A dimmer lets one light do several jobs, bright for cleaning and soft for relaxing. Check that your bulbs are marked as dimmable.

Lighting guide

Pick the right warmth

Colour temperature, measured in Kelvin, changes how a room feels more than almost anything else. Here's a quick way to choose.

2700K

Warm white

A soft, golden glow like candlelight. Relaxing and cosy, it flatters skin tones and wood finishes.

Best for: living rooms, bedrooms, dining
3000K

Soft white

Still warm but a little crisper. A good all-rounder when one bulb has to do several jobs.

Best for: kitchens, hallways, bathrooms
4000K

Cool white

Bright and neutral, closer to daylight. Keeps you alert and shows true colours for detailed tasks.

Best for: desks, workshops, reading
Room by room

Layer your light

The best-lit rooms mix three layers: ambient light to fill the space, task light to work by, and accent light for mood.

Living room

Aim for several low pools of light, not one bright ceiling fitting.

  • A floor lamp beside the sofa for reading
  • Table lamps at eye level to soften corners
  • Dimmable smart bulbs for film nights

Bedroom

Keep it warm and gentle, especially in the hour before sleep.

  • Bedside lamps with 2700K bulbs
  • Shades that hide the bulb from view
  • A sunrise routine on smart bulbs

Kitchen & dining

Bright where you prepare food, softer where you eat.

  • Pendants hung 75–85 cm above the table
  • Soft white light over worktops
  • A dimmer to switch from cooking to dinner

Home office

Reduce glare and eye strain with light from the side.

  • A desk lamp on your non-writing side
  • 4000K for focus during the day
  • Background light so the screen isn't the brightest thing
FAQ

Good questions

How do I choose the right size pendant?

Add the length and width of your room in metres and multiply by 10. The result is a good diameter in centimetres for a central pendant. Over a dining table, pick a shade about half to two-thirds the table's width.

What is CRI, and does it matter?

CRI (colour rendering index) shows how naturally a light displays colours, on a scale up to 100. Look for 90 or above in kitchens, bathrooms and anywhere you want food, skin and fabrics to look true to life.

Do smart bulbs need a hub?

Many don't. Most modern smart bulbs connect directly over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth and work with the major voice assistants. A hub mainly helps if you want to control a large number of bulbs reliably.

Can I fit a pendant myself?

Plug-in pendants and lamps need no wiring. Hard-wired ceiling fittings should be installed by a qualified electrician for safety and to meet local regulations.

What does lumens mean?

Lumens measure how much light a bulb gives out. As a rough guide, 400–500 lumens suits a bedside lamp, 800 a main living room light, and 1,000 or more a kitchen or workspace.
